CUPERTINO, Calif., 24 January 1984 — Apple Computer today introduced the Macintosh, a $2,495 desktop machine that replaces typed commands with a screen full of pictures and a device you move with your hand.

Steve Jobs unveiled it at the company's annual shareholders' meeting at the Flint Center, pointing first to a canvas bag on a table at the centre of the stage and telling the audience that everything they were about to see was coming out of it. He then lifted the machine from the bag, took a floppy disk from his jacket pocket, and put it in.

The last thing he said before the demonstration was that he would like, for the first time, to let the Macintosh speak for itself. It did.

The machine carries 128 kilobytes of memory, a Motorola 68000 processor, a nine-inch black-and-white screen of 512 by 342 pixels, and one 3.5-inch floppy drive. It ships with a word processor, a drawing program and a mouse.

Roughly 70,000 were sold by the first week of May.
